## Development Roadmap

Version: 1.0

Status: Active

---

# Purpose

This roadmap defines the long-term development direction of the Everest Investment Operating System (EIOS).

It aligns engineering, research, and institutional development into a single execution plan.

The roadmap is reviewed at every major release and updated only when strategic priorities change.

---

# Long-Term Mission

Build the world's first Institutional Intelligence Platform capable of:

- Understanding businesses
- Evaluating management quality
- Assessing capital allocation
- Valuing companies
- Allocating capital
- Learning continuously
- Discovering new business knowledge

---

# Roadmap Philosophy

The roadmap is organized around **institutional capabilities**, not software features.

Each release should leave EIOS measurably more intelligent, more explainable, and more maintainable than the previous release.

Feature count is never a success metric.

Institutional capability is.
---

# Development Principles

The EIOS roadmap is governed by the following principles.

## 1. Capability Before Features

Releases are planned around institutional capabilities, not individual software features.

A release is considered successful only if it meaningfully improves EIOS's ability to understand, reason, decide, or learn.

---

## 2. Platform Before Expansion

The platform must remain stable before introducing major new capabilities.

Architecture quality takes priority over feature quantity.

---

## 3. Knowledge Before Automation

Knowledge models must exist before automation.

EIOS should understand a problem before attempting to automate its solution.

---

## 4. Evidence Before Intelligence

Every intelligent capability must be supported by structured evidence.

Reasoning without evidence is not acceptable.

---

## 5. Documentation Before Complexity

As complexity increases, documentation quality must increase proportionally.

Major capabilities require corresponding documentation before they are considered complete.

---

## 6. Continuous Validation

Every major capability must be validated using real companies, historical data, and documented evidence before it becomes part of the institutional workflow.

---

## 7. Institutional Growth

The objective of every release is to improve one or more of the following:

- Observation
- Evidence
- Knowledge
- Reasoning
- Decision Quality
- Learning
- Explainability
- Maintainability

If a proposed feature improves none of these areas, it should not be added to the roadmap.
---

# Strategic Risks & Assumptions

The EIOS roadmap is based on a set of long-term assumptions regarding engineering discipline, research quality, and institutional development.

These assumptions should be reviewed periodically and updated when necessary.

---

# Strategic Risks

## Risk 1 – Architectural Drift

Description

As EIOS grows, there is a risk that new modules bypass the established architecture, leading to inconsistent design and increasing technical debt.

Mitigation

- Architecture reviews before major implementation.
- Maintain the Repository-first architecture.
- Update architecture documentation whenever structural changes occur.

---

## Risk 2 – Knowledge Quality

Description

Poorly validated knowledge can reduce the reliability of reasoning and decision-making.

Mitigation

- Require evidence for all institutional knowledge.
- Maintain Knowledge Objects and Evidence Objects.
- Validate important conclusions using multiple sources where possible.

---

## Risk 3 – Documentation Drift

Description

Documentation may become inconsistent with the implementation.

Mitigation

- Documentation is updated as part of every completed capability.
- PROJECT_STATE.md is reviewed every sprint.
- CHANGELOG.md is updated every release.

---

## Risk 4 – Technical Debt

Description

Temporary implementation shortcuts may accumulate and reduce maintainability.

Mitigation

- Record known debt in PROJECT_STATE.md.
- Schedule technical debt reduction during platform-focused releases.
- Avoid introducing new debt without documenting it.

---

## Risk 5 – Scope Expansion

Description

Adding too many new ideas before existing capabilities mature may reduce overall quality.

Mitigation

- Prioritize completing existing capabilities.
- Evaluate new ideas against the Development Principles.
- Focus on institutional maturity over feature count.

---

## Strategic Assumptions

The roadmap assumes:

- The institutional architecture remains the foundation of EIOS.
- Knowledge quality is more important than implementation speed.
- Long-term maintainability is prioritized over rapid expansion.
- Business reasoning remains evidence-based.
- Documentation continues to evolve with the codebase.
- Institutional capabilities mature progressively over multiple releases.

---

# Roadmap Review Policy

The roadmap should be reviewed:

- At the completion of every major release.
- When significant architectural decisions are made.
- When strategic priorities change.
- During annual project reviews.

Minor implementation changes should not require roadmap updates.

---

# Guiding Principle

The roadmap is intended to provide long-term direction.

It should remain stable, while allowing individual releases and implementation details to evolve.
